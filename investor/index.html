01The gap

Built to be seen. Impossible to hear.

Professional DJ software was designed entirely around the eye — a screen full of waveforms, meters, and crates. For a blind DJ, that design choice is a locked door.

  • Two elements out of a hundred and twenty-four.That's what a screen reader can read across Serato DJ Pro's whole interface — decks, effects, the library, the crates, all of it silent except for two controls.
  • It used to work, then it didn't.Serato was screen-reader usable through version 1.7. The 1.8 interface rebuild broke that access outright, and in the years since, it has never been restored.
  • A blind DJ can't map a controller by sight.Every knob, pad, and fader on a DJ controller looks the same by touch alone — without a way to hear what a control is, hardware itself becomes the wall.
  • djay Pro fares no better on its own.Its accessibility tree publishes only what Apple's system happens to expose, which stops well short of what a DJ actually needs mid-set: live effect state, deck timing, the crate you're standing in.
02What AccessBridge changes

Reads from the inside. Drives from the keyboard.

AccessBridge reads Serato's live internal state directly — decks, time left, key, BPM, cues, effects, the crates — read-only, no screen capture, no fake clicks. djay it reads through the Mac's own accessibility system. Nothing is guessed, and nothing claims a state the software never actually reads.
