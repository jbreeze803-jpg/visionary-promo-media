03The market & the moment

Overdue, underserved, and right on time.

  • Accessibility is now law, and the clock just moved.The European Accessibility Act has been in force across the EU since June 2025 (WCAG 2.1 AA). In the US, the DOJ's ADA Title II rule just extended its own deadline — large public entities now have until April 26, 2027, smaller ones until April 26, 2028 — while 3,117 federal web-accessibility lawsuits were filed in 2025 alone, up 27% year over year.
  • The scale is real and still growing.The World Health Organization estimates at least 2.2 billion people live with a vision impairment, roughly 43 million of them blind. The assistive-technology market itself is forecast to reach $31.22 billion by 2030.
  • Disabled founders are underfunded, which is the opportunity.Founders with disabilities are cited as up to 400% less likely to secure investment than their peers — not because the businesses are weaker, but because the pattern-matching is broken. Dedicated capital is only now emerging for this exact founder profile — Adaptation Ventures, a $10M pre-seed angel fund launched 2025, and Ability Lane, a matchmaking platform connecting disability-tech founders to investors, launched May 2026. An investor who moves first here isn't taking a risk on an unproven category — they're getting ahead of one.

Built for the blind. Better for everyone.

The same engine that names a controller by ear is a powerful hands-free layer for any professional — eyes on the work, not the screen. That is how accessibility companies grow past their first market: the technology is universal, the empathy is the moat.

A comparable disability-tech pre-seed — Accessercise, a fitness app for people with impairments — raised a £600,000 pre-seed round. Visionary Software is proposing a larger raise because it is proposing more: three shipped-or-shipping products, not one.
